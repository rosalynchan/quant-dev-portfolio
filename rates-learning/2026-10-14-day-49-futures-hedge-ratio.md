# Converting Quote DV01 into a Futures Hedge Ratio

## Abstract

A market-quote DV01 is useful only when it can be translated into an executable hedge. For a short-rate futures hedge, that translation requires more than dividing two positive numbers: portfolio and contract sensitivities must share the same rate coordinate, sign convention, bump methodology, currency, and snapshot. This article derives the signed hedge ratio, handles whole-contract rounding, and turns the calculation into a controlled front-office recommendation workflow.

## Desk Context

Yesterday's bump-and-rebootstrap process produced a portfolio sensitivity of

$$
R_P=-GBP\ 11{,}260/bp
$$

to a selected futures-adjusted quote. One short futures contract has an effective sensitivity on the same coordinate of

$$
R_H=+GBP\ 24.75/bp.
$$

The trader asks how many contracts to sell and what risk will remain after rounding. The question sounds elementary, but production errors commonly arise from confusing futures-price moves with implied-rate moves, tick value with DV01, or theoretical quantities with executable whole contracts.

## The Signed Hedge Equation

Let $R_P$ be portfolio signed DV01, $R_H$ be signed DV01 per hedge contract, and $n$ be the number of contracts. Post-hedge risk is

$$
R_{residual}=R_P+nR_H.
$$

Setting the theoretical residual to zero gives

$$
n^*=-\frac{R_P}{R_H}.
$$

The sign convention is essential. Short-rate futures are commonly quoted as

$$
Price=100-ImpliedRate.
$$

When the underlying rate rises, the futures price falls. A long futures position therefore tends to lose value and has negative sensitivity to a rate-up move; a short futures position tends to gain and has positive sensitivity.

In this article DV01 means value change when the underlying rate rises by one basis point. The portfolio has negative DV01, so it requires positive rate-up sensitivity: a short futures position.

An API must not publish an unlabelled value such as `24.75`. It should state whether the sensitivity belongs to a long or short contract, whether the coordinate is futures price or implied rate, and whether the unit is currency per basis point or currency per tick.

## Contract BPV and Effective DV01

For a simple short-rate futures contract, a textbook basis-point value can be approximated by

$$
BPV_{contract}
\approx
ContractNotional\times AccrualFraction\times10^{-4}.
$$

With GBP 1 million notional and a 0.25 accrual fraction,

$$
1{,}000{,}000\times0.25\times10^{-4}
=GBP\ 25/bp.
$$

The example uses GBP 24.75/bp to represent an effective signed DV01 after mapping the contract through the target curve, timing, or convexity methodology. Production hedge calculations should generally use contract risk generated by the same risk engine and market snapshot as the portfolio, rather than a permanently hard-coded textbook BPV.

Exchange tick value remains separate reference data. A tick is the cash value of a minimum price increment. DV01 is the value change for a one-basis-point move in an underlying rate coordinate. They can be converted only under the correct contract-specific price-rate mapping.

## Worked Numerical Example

The theoretical contract count is

$$
\begin{aligned}
n^*
&=-\frac{-11{,}260}{24.75}\\
&=454.9495.
\end{aligned}
$$

Because $R_H$ was defined for one short contract, the recommendation is approximately 454.95 short contracts.

Futures trade in whole contracts. Under a nearest-integer rule,

$$
n=455.
$$

The rounded residual is

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{residual}
&=-11{,}260+455\times24.75\\
&=+GBP\ 1.25/bp.
\end{aligned}
$$

The hedge efficiency relative to the original target-bucket risk is

$$
1-\frac{|1.25|}{11{,}260}
\approx99.989\%.
$$

The recommendation should still show its complete bridge:

- theoretical quantity: 454.9495 short contracts;
- executable quantity: 455 short contracts;
- risk before hedge: -GBP 11,260/bp;
- hedge risk: +GBP 11,261.25/bp;
- residual risk: +GBP 1.25/bp.

Rounding down to 454 contracts would leave

$$
-11{,}260+454\times24.75=-GBP\ 23.50/bp.
$$

Nearest-integer rounding is clearly better here. However, a desk may forbid residual sign reversal or require an under-hedge-only policy. Rounding is therefore an explicit business rule, not merely a formatting operation.

## When the Ratio Becomes Misleading

First, the portfolio and contract risk must share the same coordinate. A portfolio measured against an adjusted forward quote cannot be divided safely by a contract sensitivity measured against raw futures price without an explicit mapping.

Second, snapshots must match. Combining a 09:00 portfolio risk with a prior-close contract DV01 introduces market moves and aging into the ratio.

Third, one futures contract covers only its relevant region of the curve. A nearly zero target-bucket residual does not eliminate other tenor, discounting, basis, convexity, or nonlinear risks. The output should be described as a single-bucket hedge, not a fully hedged portfolio.

Finally, DV01 is a local approximation. After rounding—and especially after actual execution—the combined portfolio and filled hedge should be re-risked through the authoritative engine.

## Engineering Design

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass
from decimal import Decimal, ROUND_HALF_UP


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class FuturesHedgeRequest:
    portfolio_dv01: Decimal
    short_contract_dv01: Decimal
    residual_limit: Decimal
    portfolio_snapshot_id: str
    contract_risk_snapshot_id: str


def recommend_futures_hedge(x: FuturesHedgeRequest) -> dict:
    if x.short_contract_dv01 <= 0:
        raise ValueError("short-contract DV01 must be positive")
    if x.portfolio_snapshot_id != x.contract_risk_snapshot_id:
        raise ValueError("risk snapshot mismatch")

    theoretical = -x.portfolio_dv01 / x.short_contract_dv01
    contracts = theoretical.quantize(
        Decimal("1"), rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP
    )
    residual = x.portfolio_dv01 + contracts * x.short_contract_dv01

    return {
        "direction": "SHORT" if contracts > 0 else "LONG",
        "theoretical_contracts": theoretical,
        "tradable_contracts": abs(contracts),
        "residual_dv01": residual,
        "status": (
            "ACCEPTABLE"
            if abs(residual) <= x.residual_limit
            else "RESIDUAL_BREACH"
        ),
    }
```

## Data Quality and Production Controls

The most important controls are:

- Align portfolio and contract risk by quote coordinate, bump method, currency, basis-point unit, and snapshot.
- Source contract multiplier, tick size, expiry, and exchange code from versioned reference data.
- Recalculate residual after whole-contract rounding.
- Exclude expired, illiquid, or ineligible contracts from recommendations.
- Re-risk using actual filled quantity after execution.

A classic failure is treating exchange tick value as DV01. Tick value describes one minimum price move; DV01 describes a one-basis-point underlying-rate move. Conflating them can produce a plausible but incorrectly scaled hedge.

Focused observability should include stale contract-reference data, snapshot mismatches, theoretical-versus-filled quantity, target-bucket residual, and rejected recommendations by reason.

## Requirement Discovery and Interview Questions

When a trader says “hedge it with futures,” clarify:

- Is the target one quote bucket or total parallel DV01?
- Is portfolio risk signed as PV change for a rate-up move?
- Is contract sensitivity defined for a long or short position?
- Which expiry is eligible, and may multiple contracts be used?
- Is rounding nearest, under-hedge-only, or limit-aware?
- What residual tolerance applies, and may the residual sign flip?
- What liquidity, position-limit, and execution-window constraints apply?
- Is the recommendation based on live or official-close risk?
- Should partial fills trigger an automatic residual refresh?

A strong interview answer solves with signed sensitivities, treats contract metadata as versioned input, separates theoretical and executable quantities, and re-risks after rounding or execution.

## Key Takeaways

- A futures hedge ratio is the negative portfolio signed DV01 divided by signed DV01 per contract.
- Futures price and implied rate move in opposite directions, so direction labels are mandatory.
- Contract tick value and underlying-rate DV01 are related but not interchangeable.
- Whole-contract rounding requires a fresh residual-risk check under an explicit policy.
- A flat target bucket is not a fully hedged portfolio; remaining curve, basis, and nonlinear risks must stay visible.
